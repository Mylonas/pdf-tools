PDF or JPG: which should you send?

They're not interchangeable. The right choice depends on whether you're sending a document or a picture — and what the other person will do with it.

The quick rule

Send a PDF for anything that's a document — multi-page files, forms, contracts, invoices, CVs, anything that should print cleanly and look the same on every device. Send a JPG for a single photo or image — a picture, a screenshot, something going into a social post or a chat.

When PDF wins

  • Multiple pages in one tidy file — JPGs can't hold several pages.
  • Consistent layout — a PDF looks identical everywhere; fonts and spacing don't shift.
  • Selectable text — readers can search, copy, or run OCR.
  • Printing — PDFs are built for accurate, page-sized output.
  • Forms — fillable fields and signatures live in PDFs, not images.

When JPG wins

  • A single photo where layout doesn't matter.
  • Social media and chats that preview images but not PDFs.
  • Quick previews — a JPG shows instantly without a PDF viewer.
  • Editing in an image app that doesn't open PDFs.

Convert either way, privately

Whichever you need, meldpdf converts in your browser without uploading:

  • Photos or scans to send as a document → JPG to PDF.
  • A PDF page you want to post as an image → PDF to JPG.

Frequently asked questions

Is a PDF or JPG smaller?

It depends on content. A text document is tiny as a PDF; a detailed photo is often smaller as a JPG. For shrinking a heavy PDF, see reduce PDF size.

Can I put several JPGs into one file?

Yes — combine them into a single PDF with JPG to PDF.

Does converting upload my file?

No. Conversion runs locally in your browser. See our privacy policy.
