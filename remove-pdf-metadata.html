What metadata is hiding in your PDF

Beyond the visible pages, most PDFs carry a set of hidden properties: the author's name, the title and subject, the software and version used to create and export it, and the creation and modification dates. None of it shows on the page, but anyone can read it in a file's "Document Properties" — and it often reveals more than the sender intended.

Why it's worth removing

  • Job applications — a CV can quietly carry a previous employer's template name or your full account name.
  • Anonymity — journalists, researchers and whistleblowers may need a document with no author trail.
  • Legal and business — dates and software details can disclose how and when a document was really prepared.
  • Simple privacy — there's rarely a reason to hand strangers your name and toolchain.

How to use it

Add your PDF and the tool shows you exactly what metadata it found, then gives you a downloaded copy with those fields cleared. Doing this as the last step before sharing — after any editing — makes sure nothing you changed re-introduces old properties. For sensitive files, pair it with a password.

What it removes — and what it can't

It clears the document's information fields (title, author, subject, keywords, creator and producer). It does not alter the visible content, and it is not redaction: text and images you can see on the page remain, including anything sensitive there. It also can't strip camera EXIF data embedded inside pictures that were placed in the PDF. In short, it removes the document's hidden properties, not its content — for that, remove the pages or the material itself first.

Frequently asked questions

What metadata does a PDF contain?

Commonly the title, author, subject, keywords, the creating and producing software, and creation/modification dates.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Metadata is read and stripped in your browser; the file is never uploaded.

Why remove metadata before sharing?

It can reveal your name, organisation or software. Removing it protects your privacy when a document goes to others.
