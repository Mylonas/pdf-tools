How it works

  1. Add your PDF. Drop it in or click to choose it.
  2. Choose quality. Higher quality means larger, sharper PNGs.
  3. Download. Each page is saved as a PNG image.

PNG or JPG — which should you pick?

Both turn each PDF page into an image, but they suit different content. PNG is lossless: it reproduces sharp edges, text and lines perfectly, which makes it the right choice for documents, diagrams, screenshots and anything with fine detail. JPG (via PDF to JPG) uses lossy compression that's smaller for photographs but can blur crisp edges. Rule of thumb: PNG for pages that are mostly text or graphics, JPG for pages that are mostly photos.

What you can do with the images

  • Drop a page into a slide or document — paste a PNG straight into PowerPoint, Google Slides, Word or an email.
  • Post a page online — share a single page on the web or social media without linking the whole PDF.
  • Grab a figure or chart — export the page, then crop to the part you need.
  • Make thumbnails or previews of a document.

Quality and resolution

Higher quality renders each page at a larger pixel size — sharper, but bigger files. Standard is fine for on-screen use; choose High or Very high when the image will be printed, zoomed, or projected. Every page is exported as a separate PNG so you can keep just the ones you need.

Good to know

The result is a picture of the page, so the text in it is no longer selectable or searchable — if you need the words, use extract text instead. Pages render on a white background matching the original PDF. Rendering happens on your device; your file is never uploaded.

Frequently asked questions

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Pages are rendered to PNG in your browser and never uploaded.

PNG or JPG — which should I pick?

PNG is lossless and best for text, diagrams and screenshots; JPG is smaller and fine for photos.

Do PNGs have a transparent background?

Pages render on a white background, matching how the PDF looks.
