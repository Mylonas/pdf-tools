Is it safe to upload PDFs to online tools?

It depends entirely on where your file goes. Here's what actually happens behind most "free online PDF" sites — and how to avoid the risk.

What "upload" really means

With most online PDF converters, clicking "upload" sends your document to the company's server, where it is processed and then offered back for download. During that round trip your file leaves your device and sits, however briefly, on someone else's computer. For a holiday photo that's fine. For a contract, payslip, bank statement, medical record or ID scan, it means a copy of sensitive data has travelled across the internet to a third party.

The real risks

  • Retention: many services keep files for a period (often "deleted after 1 hour", but you're trusting that claim).
  • Data handling: your document may pass through logs, backups, or sub-processors you can't see.
  • Breach exposure: anything stored on a server can, in principle, be exposed if that server is compromised.
  • Jurisdiction: the server may be in a country with different privacy laws than yours.

How to tell if a tool uploads your file

  • Watch for a progress bar labelled "uploading" — that's your file leaving.
  • Check the privacy policy for words like "we store", "our servers", "retained for".
  • Try it offline: a genuinely browser-based tool keeps working with your Wi-Fi switched off after the page has loaded.

The safer alternative: browser-based processing

Some modern tools — including meldpdf — do all the work inside your browser using JavaScript and WebAssembly. Your file is read into the page's memory on your own device, processed there, and handed back as a download. It is never sent anywhere. There's no server that receives it, so there's nothing to retain, log or breach. That makes it appropriate for exactly the sensitive documents you shouldn't upload elsewhere.

You can confirm it yourself: load meldpdf, disconnect from the internet, and the tools still work.

Frequently asked questions

Are paid PDF services safer than free ones?

Not necessarily — paid services often still process on their servers. "Where does the file go" matters more than "is it paid".

Is browser-based processing slower?

Usually it's faster, because there's no upload/download round trip — the work happens instantly on your device.

Does meldpdf ever see my files?

No. There is no meldpdf server that receives your documents. See our privacy policy.
